ALT TEXT (full, for a long description or caption):
What researchers find when gambling moves onto phones. Research and help-seeking data from outside DC. A grid of nine findings in three rows. Money: +25 to 30% bankruptcy risk, at the high end, three to four years after a state's online sports betting launch (Online sports betting, about 7M consumers); +8% debt in collections, approximate rise in the average amount owed after online sports betting became legal (Online sports betting, about 7M consumers); minus $0.99 investment/savings, for every $1 bet on sports online. Among strapped households, card debt and overdrafts rise (Online sports betting, bank transactions). Risk, health and who pays: 5 times problem-gambling rate, among people who played online slots in the past year vs. all gamblers in Great Britain (Association, Great Britain, 2023 survey); 15 times suicide mortality, vs. the general population, among Swedish patients aged 20 to 74 with diagnosed gambling disorder (Association, Swedish clinical cohort); 60% from problem gamblers, of French online and account-based gambling revenue came from the 8.7% of players flagged as likely problem gamblers (Online + account-based gambling, France, H2 2025). Problem gambling: 10 times gambling-disorder rate, among New Jersey gamblers who bet only online (11.3%) vs. only in person (1.1%) (Association, New Jersey, 2020 to 21 survey); +87% helpline calls, and texts to Connecticut's problem-gambling helpline, Nov. 2021 vs. Nov. 2020 (Online casino + sports betting, Connecticut); 50%+ online-gambling calls, of calls to Pennsylvania's problem-gambling helpline involved problematic online gambling (Online casino + sports betting, Pennsylvania). None of these are DC estimates. The financial findings cover sports betting; few studies isolate online casino games. The British, New Jersey and Swedish results compare groups of people and do not show that gambling caused the differences.
